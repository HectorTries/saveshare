My Entire Investment Portfolio at Age 24

"When I started my TikTok page last June, I had £50,000 in savings."

By Tori | November 16, 2025

Mia Rose, 24, has become one of the UK's most-followed financial educators on TikTok. We sat down with her to discuss exactly what's in her portfolio — and how she grew from £50,000 to nearly £160,000 in just over a year.

"I can't believe I'm revealing this," she laughs, "but this is my entire investment portfolio at age 24. Now obviously this video is not to brag." She's quick to clarify her motivation: "I originally got into saving and investing because I watched other people show their portfolios. So I think that it can be really beneficial to have this level of transparency."

The Breakdown

Stocks & Shares ISA£80,122
General Investment Account£41,353
Lifetime ISA£19,886
SIPP£11,060
Workplace Pension£5,548
TOTAL£157,969

The ISA is her largest holding. "If you're not from the UK, this is a bit like a Roth IRA or TFSA — but basically it's a tax-efficient account where you can deposit £20,000 into it every year. Any gains are not subject to capital gains, income or dividend tax." She notes most is "invested in ETFs. I have some individual stocks but they make up a small percentage of the portfolio."

The general investment account holds "tax money" — £41,353, with £21,000 in a global ETF outside the ISA wrapper. "I don't like doing outside of an ISA, but I don't really have a choice" given ISA contribution limits.

The Lifetime ISA at £19,886 sits in her retirement strategy. "This is a government-backed scheme created to help first-time buyers get onto the property ladder. It is a controversial account because of the restrictions that we have — we can only buy property up to the value of £450K, which is difficult for people in London. But I would say it's so worth it for the £1,000 that you get." She's planning to use it as "a pension pot if I don't end up using it to buy my first home."

Her pension holdings include both SIPP (£11,060) and workplace pension (£5,548).

The grand total is coming to £157,969, which is totally insane.

She traces her journey back: "When I started my TikTok page last June, I had £50,000 in savings. So it has absolutely skyrocketed — and I could only attribute that to increasing my income through one a side hustle which then turned into my full-time job."

Mia Rose posts career and finance content on TikTok as @miarosemcgrath.
