# Sophie Blank: The Audacious Girl With 88% in One Fund

*SaveShare profile article — Sophie Blank*

---

Sophie Blank describes herself, with great confidence, as an audacious girl with big goals. The goals, for the record, are these: become a millionaire in her thirties, hit CoastFIRE in her forties. *CoastFIRE* — for anyone not in the Reddit deep-end — is the moment your invested pot is large enough that, even if you stopped contributing tomorrow, compound growth alone would carry you to a traditional retirement age. Most people don't get there until their fifties. Sophie plans to be there before she's 45.

She's 28. She holds a Trading 212 ISA, a LISA, and a second stocks-and-shares ISA with a different provider, and the £9,200 she shows on screen in her portfolio breakdown video is just one slice of that stack. The number is the smaller half of the story. The portfolio design is the rest of it.

There are three holdings. That's it. No individual stocks. No crypto. No thematic ETFs. No thematic anything.

- **88% — Vanguard FTSE All-World (VWRL).** The "mother ETF," in Sophie's words. The whole global economy, in one ticker.
- **6% — Vanguard FTSE Emerging Markets (VFEG).** The same logic as the All-World, except pointed at a smaller, faster-growing slice of it.
- **5.3% — iShares Physical Gold (SGLN).** A small gold allocation because gold has had, in Sophie's understated phrasing, "an absolute run."

That last one is doing more work than the number suggests. Five-point-three percent of £9,200 is £487. It's the price of a decent night out. In portfolio-theory terms, it's a tail-risk hedge — a small position in something that doesn't move with the rest of the holdings, bought because if the world ever does the dramatic thing, gold historically does the opposite dramatic thing. Most twenty-eight-year-olds don't bother. Most twenty-eight-year-olds with five months of investing behind them don't bother even more. Sophie, who has £487 of gold and a confident tone, bothers.

The numbers around her performance are the bit that grabs you. Five months of investing, started at the beginning of the tax year. £9,200 in the pot. £706.60 of profit. Thirty-nine percent, before you've finished your coffee. The temptation, watching the video, is to assume Sophie is some kind of investing savant. She's careful — explicitly, repeatedly — to say she isn't. *"I'm not a financial advisor,"* she says, twice, in the same breath. *"Please do your own research."* She knows what 39% in five months actually is: a strong tailwind, a steady hand on the tiller, and a small pot benefiting from the maths of percentage gains on a low base. She says so without spoiling the moment. That restraint is what makes her watchable.

The portfolio design, when you sit with it for a minute, is doing something quite specific. Two of the three holdings — VWRL and VFEG — are global equity index funds. They're not really two bets. They're one bet, with the VFEG acting as a deliberate tilt toward emerging markets. The gold position is the only thing in the portfolio that doesn't move with equities. The whole structure is, in three line items, the cleanest expression of a long-term, capital-growth philosophy you can build inside a £20,000-a-year ISA wrapper: own the world, tilt a little, hold a sliver of gold for the bad days, and don't touch it.

What Sophie has actually built, without quite saying it that way, is a portfolio that lets her sleep. There are no daily decisions to make. No earnings reports to read. No individual stocks to second-guess. The hardest decision she has to make in any given month is whether to top up the ISA or wait until payday. That's the gift of the design — and the discipline of it shows in the way she talks about the future. Millionaire by thirty-something. CoastFIRE by forty-something. The plan has the same clean three-asset shape as the portfolio. Hold the line. Let the maths do the work.

The thing Sophie will have to learn, eventually, is that 39% doesn't repeat. It can't. If the FTSE All-World does another 39% in the next twelve months, Sophie will probably do 35%, give or take the drag from her gold and EM tilts, and the years after that will be lower — possibly much lower, possibly boring enough that she'll forget to refresh the app. That's the part the video doesn't prepare you for. The TikTok doesn't have a clip for *year three of the bull market, when nothing happens and you have to leave it anyway.*

She'll learn that too. She has that energy.

For now, she refreshes the app. £9,200. Plus £706.60. The audacious girl with 88% in one fund checks her phone.
